An AI roadmap typically serves two main purposes: it either outlines a learning path for individuals to master the technology or a strategic plan for businesses to implement AI at scale. [1, 2]
1. AI Learning Roadmap (Individual)
For those looking to become an AI Engineer or Data Scientist in 2026, the path is generally divided into several progressive phases: [1, 2]
• Phase 1: Foundations (Months 1–2)
	o Mathematics: Focus on Linear Algebra (matrices), Probability/Statistics, and Calculus (gradients).
	o Programming: Master Python, including libraries like NumPy, Pandas, and Matplotlib.
	• Phase 2: Machine Learning (Months 3–6)
	o Learn Supervised (Regression, Classification) and Unsupervised (Clustering) learning.
	o Understand model evaluation metrics like accuracy, precision, and recall.
	• Phase 3: Deep Learning & Generative AI (Months 7–10)
	o Neural Networks: Study CNNs (for vision) and Transformers (for text) using frameworks like PyTorch or TensorFlow.
	o Generative AI: Explore Large Language Models (LLMs), Prompt Engineering, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).
	• Phase 4: Engineering & Deployment (Months 11–12)
	o Learn to build AI Agents and deploy models using Docker and cloud platforms like AWS or Google Cloud. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]
2. Business Implementation Roadmap (Enterprise)
	Organizations use AI roadmaps to ensure initiatives align with business goals rather than just deploying technology for its own sake. Key workstreams include: [1]
	• AI Strategy & Value: Defining specific use cases that will drive ROI.
	• Data Readiness: Ensuring high-quality, "AI-ready" data is available and governed.
	• Governance & Ethics: Establishing policies for safety, transparency, and regulatory compliance.
	• Workforce Readiness: Redesigning roles and upskilling employees for human-AI collaboration. [1, 2]
